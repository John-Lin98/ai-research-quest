## Short project description

Research Quest is an open-source gamified research Skill. The AI reads documents and existing discussion first, maps explicit knowledge, unspoken experience, open questions, and hidden risks, then asks one high-value question per turn by default. Users can answer, ask the AI a question, or proactively add documents, constraints, preferences, deadlines, results, and corrections. Confirmed decisions accumulate in a traceable Context and Goal before execution is handed to Codex or another agent.

The same method can be remixed into an RPG, detective story, card game, management simulation, or exploration Skill. Models such as Sol, Fable, or Kimi can assist with characters, narrative, levels, rewards, and interaction copy.

Stars, follows, and practical feedback are welcome.

## Five-post X thread

### 1/5

I turned research-task clarification into an open-source game Skill: Research Quest.

It does not merely add points to chat. It uses game-like, small-step interaction to understand the user, then hands a sufficiently complete Context and Goal to Codex or another agent. 🧵

### 2/5

The core has two parts:

1. a Known–Unknown cognition map;
2. grill-me-with-docs: read the documents first, then ask one question that the material cannot answer and that can materially change the result.

Users can answer, ask questions, or add task clues proactively.

### 3/5

Why one question per turn and restrained answers?

For the user: less effort understanding a questionnaire and a long generic response.

For the AI: less Context inflation and fewer unconfirmed assumptions contaminating later reasoning and Goal updates.

### 4/5

The game style is not fixed. Keep the cognition map and document-first alignment, then build an RPG, detective story, card game, management simulation, or exploration Skill.

Models such as Sol, Fable, or Kimi can help draft characters, narrative, levels, rewards, and interaction copy.

## Release boundary

- Do not describe the public AlphaFold2 pilot as a completed research experiment.
- Do not claim that gamification has already been proven to improve research ability, efficiency, or creativity.
- Sol, Fable, and Kimi are examples of optional creative collaborators, not the result of a unified benchmark.
- Prefer the repository's `releases/latest` link so installation instructions point to the latest release.
